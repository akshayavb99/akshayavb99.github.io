## In one line

Sequence models can drop recurrence and convolution entirely and rely on attention alone, which makes training far more parallel.

## What it proposes

- The Transformer: an encoder-decoder model built from stacked attention and feed-forward layers.
- Scaled dot-product attention, run in several heads in parallel so the model can look at different relationships at once.
- Positional encodings added to the inputs, since the model has no built-in sense of word order.
- The base model uses 6 layers, 8 heads, and a model width of 512.

## Results

- Strong machine translation results on WMT 2014 English-to-German, at a fraction of the training cost of earlier systems.
- The base model trained in roughly half a day on 8 GPUs.

## Why it stuck with me

- Nearly every model I work with now descends from this architecture, so it is worth knowing in detail.
- Attention maps give a natural entry point for interpretability work, which connects it to my AI safety reading.

## Open questions

- Attention cost grows quadratically with sequence length. How have later papers addressed this?
- How much of what attention heads appear to do is real structure, and how much is a story we tell about them?
